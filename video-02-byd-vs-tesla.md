### CHƯƠNG 1 — The battery boy from Anhui (1:45–3:45)

> In 1995, a chemist named Wang Chuanfu borrowed money from a cousin and started a battery company in Shenzhen. Twenty people. One idea: Japanese companies were selling rechargeable batteries for a fortune, and he believed he could make them for a fraction of the cost by replacing expensive robots with cheap human hands and clever jigs.
>
> He was right. Within a few years, BYD was one of the biggest phone battery makers in the world. Motorola and Nokia were customers.
>
> In 2003 he did something his investors hated. He bought a struggling state-owned car factory. The stock dropped. People asked why a battery maker wanted to build cars.
>
> His answer: an electric car is a battery with wheels. And he already made batteries.

**Hình ảnh:** ảnh tư liệu Thâm Quyến thập niên 90 (đen trắng, lower-third "Shenzhen, 1995"); công nhân lắp pin bằng tay; điện thoại Nokia/Motorola đời cũ trên bàn gỗ.

### CHƯƠNG 2 — The laugh (3:45–5:15)

> In 2008, Warren Buffett's Berkshire Hathaway bought about ten percent of BYD for roughly two hundred thirty million dollars. Charlie Munger called Wang a mix of Thomas Edison and Jack Welch.
>
> In 2011, a Bloomberg interviewer asked Elon Musk about BYD. He laughed. "Have you seen their car?"
>
> At the time, he had a point. BYD's cars were cheap, plain, and mostly ignored outside China. Tesla was about to launch the Model S and redefine what an electric car could be.
>
> For the next decade, the story wrote itself. Tesla was the future. BYD was a cheap Chinese brand.

**Hình ảnh:** lưới đen, timeline đỏ 2008 → 2011; ảnh tư liệu trụ sở Berkshire Omaha (có giấy phép); sedan đỏ cũ thập niên 2010 trên đường phố Trung Quốc.

### CHƯƠNG 3 — Owning the whole stack (5:15–7:45)

> Here's what Tesla does well: software, design, the brand. But Tesla buys most of its battery cells from Panasonic, LG and CATL.
>
> BYD makes its own. In 2020 it launched the Blade battery — long, flat lithium iron phosphate cells packed directly into the car's structure. Cheaper chemistry. No cobalt. Far less prone to catching fire.
>
> BYD also makes its own power chips through BYD Semiconductor. Its own electric motors. Its own seats, lights, and much of the electronics. Industry analysts estimate it builds a far bigger share of each car in-house than any Western automaker.
>
> When the global chip shortage hit in 2021, carmakers around the world shut down lines. BYD kept building.
>
> And when it wanted to export, it didn't wait for shipping companies. It bought and built its own fleet of car-carrying ships. The first one, the BYD Explorer No.1, sailed in 2024.
>
> Battery, chip, motor, factory, ship. One company. One country.

**Hình ảnh:** sơ đồ chuỗi giá trị kiểu video A: 5 ô ngang (Battery / Chips / Motor / Factory / Ship), bên Tesla tô xám các ô mua ngoài, bên BYD tô đỏ toàn bộ; macro cell pin; tàu chở xe rời cảng.

### CHƯƠNG 4 — The price war (7:45–9:30)

> Owning everything means one thing above all: you decide your own costs.
>
> In 2023, BYD launched the Seagull — a small electric hatchback priced in China at under ten thousand U.S. dollars. Tesla's cheapest car cost more than three times that.
>
> Then in March 2025, BYD showed a charging system it said could add about four hundred kilometers of range in five minutes. Roughly as fast as filling a gas tank.
>
> Tesla cut prices again and again. But you can only cut so far when you're buying your most expensive part from someone else.

**Hình ảnh:** hatchback nhỏ màu pastel trên phố Trung Quốc; đồng hồ đếm 5:00 → 0:00 cạnh trụ sạc; biểu đồ giá Tesla bậc thang đi xuống.

### CHƯƠNG 5 — The wall (9:30–11:00)

> So why haven't Americans seen BYD on their roads? Because in May 2024, the U.S. raised tariffs on Chinese electric cars to one hundred percent.
>
> But the rest of the world doesn't have that wall. BYD is building factories in Hungary, Brazil, Thailand and Turkey. It is selling in Europe, Latin America, Southeast Asia, Australia.
>
> Tesla can make a cheaper car. It can build a robotaxi. What it can't do quickly is rebuild a supply chain that BYD spent thirty years growing — from a battery for a flip phone to a ship that carries cars across the ocean.
